About

I'm a computational biologist with four years of oncology drug discovery experience at Kronos Bio, where I developed bioinformatics pipelines for target identification, biomarker discovery, drug-combination rationale, and patient stratification. My work spanned hematological malignancies, TNBC, SCLC, HPV-driven cervical cancer, and DLBCL, integrating multi-omic data (RNA-seq, ChIP-seq) with large-scale perturbation screens (DepMap, CCLE, PRISM) to drive therapeutic decisions.

My PhD at the University of Florida focused on computational phylogenomics, where I developed structure-aware evolutionary models and clade-specific substitution frameworks for deep metazoan phylogeny. This work produced three first-author publications and contributions to the Avian Phylogenomics Consortium.

Since leaving Kronos Bio, I've been expanding my toolkit into single-cell RNA-seq analysis (scanpy), building modular NGS pipelines in Nextflow, and exploring agentic AI systems. I'm currently looking for roles where I can apply machine learning and multi-omics integration to precision medicine, whether in industry or academia.

PRISM ML Biomarker Pipeline ★ Flagship

Feature-discovery pipeline on DepMap/PRISM that separates genuine drug-response biomarkers from tissue-of-origin proxies, using RAF inhibitor AZ-628 across 700+ cancer cell lines. BRAF serves as a positive control, not the endpoint.

Python scikit-learn SHAP PRISM DepMap
Click to expand ↓

Problem

Drug-sensitivity prediction from genomics is confounded by tissue of origin: a drug's most responsive lineage is enriched for its biomarker, so genes that merely mark that tissue correlate with response without informing mechanism. For AZ-628, melanoma is both the most BRAF-mutant and most sensitive lineage — so which features are real biology, and which are just melanoma markers?

Approach

Screened multi-omic DepMap features against PRISM sensitivity on training data only, with BRAF mutation as a positive control (Cohen's d = −1.5, p = 1.5×10⁻⁹). Ranked features by a consensus of SHAP, bootstrapped Gini, and permutation importance, then applied full-lineage residualization to strip out all tissue-of-origin signal and see which features survive.

Key Results

The top features are MAPK-pathway-output genes (ETV4, DUSP6, SPRY4) that retain ~72% of their association with sensitivity after removing all lineage — while melanocyte-identity markers collapse to ~8%, exposing them as tissue proxies. Run untuned on two more drugs, the pipeline recovers each mechanism in the top three features (BRAF for dabrafenib; the p53 program for idasanutlin).

EOPC GWAS

Case-control GWAS of 3,364 candidate germline variants for early-onset pancreatic cancer. Lead hit: PSMA8 (OR=2.61, p=9.3e-12).

Python PLINK2 GWAS Firth Regression scipy
Click to expand ↓

Problem

Identifying germline risk factors for early-onset pancreatic cancer (EOPC) from CRISPR-nominated candidate variants on chromosome 18, in a cohort of 473 EOPC patients and 891 controls.

Approach

Firth-penalized logistic regression adjusting for age, sex, BMI, and population structure (PC1-PC5). Rigorous QC (call rate, HWE, MAF filters), Bonferroni and FDR correction, sensitivity analysis with 9 covariates and multiple genetic models.

Key Results

Lead variant in PSMA8 (proteasome subunit) with OR=2.61 and genome-wide significance. Secondary hit in EPG5 (autophagy regulator). Both implicate protein homeostasis pathways, converging with CRISPR functional evidence. Genomic inflation lambda=1.004.

Ornish Proteomics

Exploratory analysis of 2,832 Olink HT proteins from the Ornish Alzheimer's lifestyle intervention trial. 33 proteins at FDR < 0.05.

Python Olink HT scipy gseapy Proteomics
Click to expand ↓

Problem

Determining whether the Ornish lifestyle intervention produces detectable molecular changes in plasma proteins of early-stage Alzheimer's patients, using proteomic data not analyzed in the published trial.

Approach

Paired-delta Wilcoxon test on within-patient change scores (intervention vs. control), eliminating time-invariant confounders. Panel-corrected ORA and pre-ranked GSEA across GO, KEGG, Reactome, Hallmark, and WikiPathways. Crossover concordance and outlier sensitivity analysis.

Key Results

33 differentially expressed proteins at FDR < 0.05 with 91-100% individual-level concordance. Dominant signal in cell adhesion and blood-brain barrier integrity pathways. Established AD markers (NEFL, GFAP, MAPT) unaffected, indicating peripheral rather than direct neuronal signal.

Phylogenomics (PhD)

Structure-aware evolutionary models and clade-specific substitution frameworks for deep metazoan phylogeny. 3 first-author publications.

Python R RAxML IQ-TREE Custom Models
Click to expand ↓

Problem

Standard substitution models assume uniform evolution across all protein sites, but different structural environments (surface, core, interface) evolve under different constraints, leading to systematic errors in deep phylogenetic inference.

Approach

Developed structure-aware substitution models that partition sites by structural environment, and trained 14 clade-specific protein substitution models across 7 deep-metazoan datasets. Used per-site likelihood diagnostics to evaluate competing topologies.

Key Results

Three first-author publications demonstrating that accounting for protein structure improves phylogenetic accuracy. Contributed to the Avian Phylogenomics Consortium.
